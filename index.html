What is an ETF?

An ETF, or exchange-traded fund, is one investment that holds many.

An ETF is similar to a mutual fund. Both let you invest in a collection of stocks or bonds. The main difference is that ETFs trade on stock exchanges like individual stocks and generally have lower fees. This makes them a simple, low-cost way to build a diversified investment portfolio.

Built-in diversification
ETFs spread your money across many investments, helping reduce risk.
Low cost
The yearly fee, called the MER, is often 0.05% to 0.25%. On $10,000 that's $5 to $25 a year.
Many flavours
Canadian stocks, US stocks, international stocks, bonds, regions, themes like clean energy, or dividend income.

The PolarisAI Navigator

Your personal investment guide. It sits beside every step and explains things in plain language.

What it does

  • Asks what matters to you, such as Canadian companies, sustainable investing, regular income or sectors they’re interested in.
  • Makes the changes you ask for. Swap a fund, add a second fund, add a thematic ETF such as real estate, or add a high-risk ETF such as cryptocurrency.
  • Tells you the trade-off first. For example: “At 5%, your ups and downs go from 9.3% to about 9.8%. Add it?”
  • Answers your questions at every step.

How it keeps you safe

  • It checks every change against your risk level.
  • It won’t add a fund that moves almost exactly like one you already have.
  • Thematic ETFs are limited to 10% each, high-risk ETFs to 5% each, and all of them together to 20%.
  • High-risk ETFs need your OK first.

The Polaris Portfolio Builder

Once you’ve chosen your funds, the Portfolio Builder decides how much of your money goes into each one.

How it works

  • It looks at each fund’s expected return, how much it rises and falls, and how it moves compared with your other funds.
  • It mixes funds that don’t move together. When stocks fall, bonds often hold steady. Mixing them smooths the ride.
  • It builds two portfolios side by side from the same funds: the steadiest mix (the smallest ups and downs) and the highest expected growth whose ups and downs stay within your risk level.
  • You choose. For Low, we recommend the steadiest mix. For Moderate and High, we recommend the highest expected growth.

Its rules

  • Each fund category gets between 5% and 40%, so no single fund dominates. Emerging markets swing more, so they’re limited to 15%.
  • Your mix of stocks and bonds stays within your risk level. A Moderate portfolio starts at 60% stocks and 40% bonds.
  • If you add a thematic or high-risk ETF, it stays at the amount you picked, such as 5%. The Portfolio Builder divides the rest of your money among your core funds.
  • Returns are estimates, not promises. A 60/40 mix is expected to earn about 5.1% a year.

This is a standard method used by investment managers, called mean-variance optimization: a minimum-variance portfolio and a maximum-return portfolio with a volatility cap.

Polaris Outlook

Polaris Outlook shows what your portfolio could be worth in 5, 10 or 20 years. No one knows how markets will move, so it shows a range, not a single number.

How it works

  • It tests 2,000 possible futures for your portfolio, each with its own mix of good and bad years.
  • It uses three methods and shows them side by side: Monte Carlo (bell-curve returns from our long-run assumptions), Bootstrap (replaying real market weeks from the last 3 years) and Hybrid (real weekly ups and downs with our long-run returns), which we use for your headline numbers.
  • It includes any money you plan to add along the way.
  • It sorts the results to show the median (middle) result, an upside and a downside.

How to read it

  • Median: half the futures end higher, half end lower.
  • Upside (95th percentile): only 1 in 20 futures end higher.
  • Downside (5th percentile): only 1 in 20 futures end lower.
  • Example: $10,000 in a 60/40 mix for 20 years: median about $25,000, upside about $50,000, downside about $13,000.

These are illustrations, not a promise. Real markets can do better or worse.

Looking ahead

When markets move, your mix drifts away from the one you chose. If it moves more than 5 percentage points, PolarisAI lets you know and suggests how to rebalance it.

When your life changes, such as a new job, a new goal or nearing retirement, update your answers and we adjust your plan. We also check in with you once a year.

The result: a portfolio built around your choices, not ours.
