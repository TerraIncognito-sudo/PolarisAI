    Satellites: 0% of a 20% limit The seven core categories always keep at least 80%.

    Why satellites are limited to 20%. Your portfolio has two parts. The core is your seven broad stock and bond funds. They hold thousands of companies and bonds, so they keep your risk spread out. Satellites are the thematic and high-risk ETFs you pick, such as real estate, infrastructure or cryptocurrency.

    Each add-on focuses on one industry, region or asset, so it can rise and fall much more than the market. Small satellites let you invest in what interests you. Large ones could push your risk past the level you chose.

    So we set three limits:

    • Thematic ETFs: up to 10% each.
    • High-risk ETFs such as cryptocurrency: up to 5% each, after you confirm you understand the risks.
    • All satellites together: up to 20%. The core always keeps at least 80%.

    This is a common approach called core and satellite: a broad, low-cost core does most of the work, and small satellites reflect your personal choices.

    Explain these results

    Two portfolios, built from the same funds

    Both use exactly the funds you chose, and both stay within your risk level. They differ only in how much goes into each fund.

    Steadiest mix (Minimum Variance) is the combination with the smallest ups and downs. It leans on the funds that move the least and on funds that don’t move together.

    Highest expected growth (Maximum Return) is the combination with the highest expected return, as long as its ups and downs stay at or below your risk limit. Your risk limit is the volatility of the boldest mix your risk level allows.

    Risk and return chart

    Every point on the curve is the best expected return for that amount of ups and downs. The dashed line is your risk limit. The two dots are your two portfolios. Tap a dot to choose it.

    Polaris Outlook and the three methods

    We test each portfolio in 2,000 possible futures, three different ways. Monte Carlo draws bell-curve returns from our long-run assumptions. Bootstrap replays real weeks from the last 3 years in random order. Hybrid uses those real weekly ups and downs, but shifts them to our long-run return assumptions. We use Hybrid for your headline numbers.

    The downside (5th percentile) is the result only 1 in 20 futures fall below. The upside (95th percentile) is the result only 1 in 20 futures beat. This is not a promise.

      How we keep your portfolio on track

      Markets move, so your mix drifts over time. Bringing it back is called rebalancing.

      1. TodayBuy your funds

        Buy the funds listed above in your brokerage account, or we can do it for you.

      2. Each time you add moneyNew money fills the gaps

        It goes to the funds furthest below their target, so your mix stays on track without selling.

      3. All yearWe watch your mix

        If it drifts more than 5 points from your choice (say, from 60% stocks to 66% or 54%), we contact you with an update and suggested trades. You decide.

      4. Once a yearYour yearly check-up

        We update your portfolio with new fund data and confirm your goals haven’t changed.

      5. Any timeYour life changes

        Update your answers and we rebuild your plan, or speak to a client advisor.
