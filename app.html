    Satellites: 0% of a 20% limit

    Why satellites are limited to 20%. Your portfolio has two parts. The core is your seven broad stock and bond funds. They hold thousands of companies and bonds, so they keep your risk spread out. Satellites are the thematic and high-risk ETFs you pick, such as real estate, infrastructure or cryptocurrency.

    Each add-on focuses on one industry, region or asset, so it can rise and fall much more than the market. Small satellites let you invest in what interests you. Large ones could push your risk past the level you chose.

    So we set three limits:

    • Thematic ETFs: up to 10% each.
    • High-risk ETFs such as cryptocurrency: up to 5% each, after you confirm you understand the risks.
    • All satellites together: up to 20%. The core always keeps at least 80%.

    This is a common approach called core and satellite: a broad, low-cost core does most of the work, and small satellites reflect your personal choices.

        How we keep your portfolio on track

        Markets move, so your mix drifts over time. Bringing it back is called rebalancing.

        1. FirstReview your plan

          Read My Investment Plan Report. If you have questions, chat with one of our licensed client advisors. They can check the plan against your full financial situation, taxes and goals.

        2. If it suits youWe buy your funds

          Approve your plan and PolarisAI buys the funds listed above for you, in your PolarisAI account, in the amounts shown.

        3. Each time you add moneyNew money fills the gaps

          We invest it in the funds furthest below their target, so your mix stays on track without selling.

        4. All yearWe watch your mix

          If it drifts more than 5 points from your choice (say, from 60% stocks to 66% or 54%), we let you know and rebalance it back to your target, with your OK.

        5. Once a yearYour yearly check-up

        How we narrow 3,106 ETFs to 650

        A fund must pass all five of these checks before the portfolio builder will use it.

        1. Sold on a Canadian or US stock exchangeSo you can buy it through an ordinary Canadian brokerage account.
        2. Big enoughAt least $50 million in the fund for Canadian-listed ETFs, and US$250 million for US-listed ones. Very small funds are more likely to close, which can force you to sell.
        3. Traded often enoughSo you can buy and sell easily, at a fair price.
        4. At least three years of pricesSo we can measure how much it rises and falls, and how it moves alongside your other funds.
        5. Complete key factsIts fee, size and other basics are in our fund database, so we can compare it fairly.

        Then, for your portfolio: the builder keeps only funds that fit your risk level, picks broad, whole-market funds in seven core categories, applies your preferences (such as Canadian-dollar or sustainable funds), and chooses the lowest-fee fund in each category.
